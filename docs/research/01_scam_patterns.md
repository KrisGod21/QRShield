**Pitch takeaway:** every regulator gives the same advice, "don't scan unknown QRs, check the payee, PIN means paying." All of it relies on the user's judgement at the moment of scanning. **No official tool inspects the QR payload before the UPI app opens.** That gap is QR Shield's niche.

## 2. Attack patterns

Background for the "detectable signal" column: a UPI QR is a deep link of the form `upi://pay?pa=<VPA>&pn=<payee name>&am=<amount>&tn=<note>&tr=<ref>&mc=<MCC>&cu=INR...`. `pa` and `pn` are mandatory, `am` is the amount, `tn` is the transaction note, and `tr` is a reference. When `mam` is absent, the amount is not editable. Source (NPCI UPI Linking Specs v1.6 draft, Nov 2017 copy): https://www.labnol.org/files/linking.pdf ; NPCI circular on deep-linking compliance: https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/upi/circular/2017/Circular18_BankCompliances_to_enbaleUPIMerchantecosystem_0.pdf. (The `mc`, `mode` and `sign` semantics are not confirmed from an NPCI primary source in this research: **UNVERIFIED**.)

### Cross-cutting payload signals (implementation checklist)

1. **Scheme classification**: `upi://pay` (payment), `upi://mandate` (autopay mandate, high risk if unexpected), `http(s)://` (URL), `intent://` / `market://` (app install), `tel:` / `sms:` / `WIFI:` (other), plain text containing a VPA.
2. **UPI field checks**: `pa` syntax (`handle@psp`) and known PSP suffix list; personal vs merchant heuristics (10-digit mobile in the local part, presence of `mc`/`tr`/`sign`); `am` prefilled and locked; very high amount; `tn`/`pn` keyword scoring (Section 4); `pn` vs `pa` vs user-stated payee mismatch; brand-in-name-but-not-in-handle; edit-distance to known official VPAs.
3. **URL checks**: shorteners, IP hosts, punycode/homoglyphs, risky TLDs, brand-in-subdomain, `.apk` paths, http without TLS, gov-impersonation (gov keywords but not `.gov.in`/`.nic.in`), domain age (optional, online).
4. **Context checks (UX)**: "What are you doing? Paying a shop / Receiving money / Paying a bill / Donating". If the user says "Receiving money", **any** `upi://pay` is a scam by definition (NPCI/RBI rule).
5. **Source checks**: QR decoded from a gallery image or screenshot (forwarded on WhatsApp) is riskier than a live camera scan of a physical sticker in "receive" scenarios. For physical stickers, OCR the printed label vs the payload.

## 4. Keyword lists (for `tn` / `pn` / URL scoring)

One labelled dataset exists (Hinglish & English financial scam text, 35 hand-written Hinglish scam patterns: KYC, UPI, lottery, loan, OTP, PAN, electricity): https://huggingface.co/bolewara/hinglish-scam-text-dataset. A Hindi spam-SMS corpus from NIT Silchar (3,894 msgs): https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/spam-sms-hindi-language. ## 5. Uncertainties and gaps

1. **Primary documents not machine-read**: The RBI BE(A)WARE PDF and RBI Kehta Hai page (bot-check / redirect), and the sansad.in PDFs for USQ 2245 (9 Dec 2024) and USQ 2481 (Dec 2025). The figures come from multiple consistent news reports of those replies (MediaNama, IANS, The420, BoomLive). Before submission, open the PDFs manually to confirm.
2. **CERT-In QR advisory**: only secondary text found; no advisory ID. **UNVERIFIED**.
3. **NPCI Safety Shield wording**: retrieved via search snippet. The page is JS-rendered and returned only its title on fetch.
4. **Verified-name display date**: sources conflict (30 Jun 2025 vs 1 Jun 2026).
5. **P2P collect discontinuation (1 Oct 2025)**: well reported from the 29 Jul 2025 circular; no post-rollout confirmation found, but no reversal reported either.
6. **"56.86 crore QRs to 6.5 crore merchants"**: not verified; use Worldline's 731.38 M (CY2025) instead.
7. **UPI share of fraud**: the only percentage found is from 2022 (>35% of all NCRP complaints). No 2024-26 share was found.
8. **RBI card/internet fraud series**: the threshold (>= Rs 1 lakh) makes it unsuitable for UPI/QR scale. FY25 amounts are inconsistent across reports, and FY26 (293 cases) looks like a reporting/classification change. Not recommended for the slide.
9. **QR-to-APK in India**: APK fraud is well documented via SMS/WhatsApp. A specific *QR-delivered* APK case in India was not found.
10. **Fake parking / EV QR in India**: the Mumbai Fort case (Aug 2026) and the Delhi MCD viral video (no official confirmation). EV-charger QR fraud is documented only in Europe.
11. **Khajuraho victim count** varies (6 / 10 / 12 shops plus a petrol pump) across reports.
12. **Keyword frequencies**: the Hindi/Hinglish/Marathi lists are compiled, not empirically measured. Validate against a sample (e.g. the Hugging Face dataset or Chakshu-style messages) before claiming accuracy.
13. **Keepnet / quishing compilations** are secondary and internally inconsistent. Prefer Kaspersky (primary press release) and APWG/Mimecast for the slide.
14. The 2,000-complaints-per-month figure for Hyderabad QR complaints (Deccan Chronicle, 2023) appeared only in a search summary and was not fetched: **UNVERIFIED**, not used.
