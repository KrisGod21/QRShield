## A. Non-URL QR payload types and their risks

### A.1 Reference formats

The de facto reference for non-URL QR payloads is the ZXing wiki "Barcode Contents" page: https://github.com/zxing/zxing/wiki/Barcode-Contents

- **WIFI**: `WIFI:T:WPA;S:mynetwork;P:mypass;;`. `T` is the auth type: `WEP`, `WPA`, `WPA2-EAP` or `nopass`, and it can be omitted for an open network. `S` is the SSID and is required. `P` is the password. `H:true` marks a hidden SSID. WPA2-EAP adds `E`, `A`, `I` and `PH2`. Field order is free. The characters `\ ; , " :` are escaped with a backslash. Older ZXing releases misused `H` for the phase-2 method. (ZXing wiki, above)
- **WPA3 additions**: the Wi-Fi Alliance WPA3 specification has a "Wi-Fi URI" ABNF that adds:
  - `R:` (Transition Disable, hex)
  - `I:` (SAE password identifier)
  - `K:` (SAE-PK public key, base64 DER SubjectPublicKeyInfo)

  This comes from a secondary reading of the spec at https://zenn.dev/link/comments/876093e0c18b65. Sources disagree on whether WPA3 codes should use `T:SAE` or `T:WPA`. Some generators emit `T:SAE`, which some phones fail to parse, and `T:WPA` is said to work across WPA/WPA2/WPA3 (https://tlinky.com/how-to-create-a-wifi-qr-code/). **UNVERIFIED against the primary Wi-Fi Alliance PDF.**
- **Wi-Fi Easy Connect (DPP)** uses a different QR payload (a DPP URI) for device provisioning (https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/stable/api-reference/network/esp_dpp.html). The exact DPP URI format is **UNVERIFIED** here.
- **tel**: `tel:+12125551212`. ZXing says readers should pre-fill the dialer, not auto-dial.
- **sms**: `sms:+18005551212` or `sms:+18005551212:body`. `SMSTO:` is seen in the wild without a formal spec. `mms:`/`MMSTO:` are similar. (ZXing wiki)
- **mailto**: `mailto:a@b.com?cc=..&bcc=..&subject=..&body=..`. `MATMSG` is NTT DoCoMo's format. ZXing gives no full syntax. The commonly seen form `MATMSG:TO:..;SUB:..;BODY:..;;` is **UNVERIFIED** from a primary spec.
- **geo**: `geo:40.71872,-73.98905,100` (latitude, longitude, altitude).
- **MECARD**: `MECARD:N:Owen,Sean;TEL:...;EMAIL:...;;`
- **vCard**: `BEGIN:VCARD ... END:VCARD`
- **vEvent**: `BEGIN:VEVENT SUMMARY:.. DTSTART:.. DTEND:.. END:VEVENT`
- **market**: `market://details?id=org.example.foo` (ZXing wiki)
- **otpauth**: `otpauth://totp/Issuer:account?secret=BASE32&issuer=Issuer`. The type is `totp` or `hotp`, and the secret is Base32. Google Authenticator supports only SHA1, 6/8 digits and a 30 s period. (https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@otplib/uri@13.4.1/README.md, https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-andesco-otpauth-uri/)
- **FIDO cross-device / passkey (hybrid)**: `FIDO:/` followed by digit-encoded CBOR. Each 7-byte chunk is written as a 17-digit base-10 number. The scheme is uppercase with a single slash. The spec text now lives in the FIDO Proximity Exchange Protocol working draft (https://fidoalliance.org/specs/hybrid/proximity-exchange-protocol-v1.0-wd-20260717.html), and CTAP 2.3 added BLE as a data channel (https://developers.yubico.com/CTAP/CTAP2.3.html).
- **EMVCo merchant-presented QR (Bharat QR)**:
  - The payload starts with `000201` and ends with `6304` plus a CRC16-CCITT-FALSE checksum.
  - Tags 26–51 hold merchant-account templates (https://docs.docbits.com/administration-and-setup/settings/document-processing/module/bar-code-qr-code-extraction/emvco-mpm).
  - Razorpay's sample Bharat QR has a template containing `0010A000000524` (sub-tag 00 = UPI AID `A000000524`) and `0117razorpaybqr@icici` (sub-tag 01 = VPA) (https://razorpay.com/docs/api/qr-codes/image-content/entity).
  - NPCI required all Bharat QRs deployed after 15 Sep 2017 to carry UPI credentials (https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/upi/circular/2017/Circular28-Scanning-integrated-Bharat-through-UPI.pdf).
  - One open-source parser uses GUI value `UPI` instead of the AID. A parser should accept both. **The exact NPCI tag-26 layout is UNVERIFIED** from the NPCI spec itself.
- **UPI mandate (AutoPay) QR**: `upi://mandate?pa=..&pn=..&mn=..&am=..&amrule=..&recur=MONTHLY&validitystart=..&validityend=..&rev=Y|N&block=..&purpose=..`. Recurrence can be `ONETIME`, `DAILY` … `YEARLY` or `ASPRESENTED`. `rev=N` (non-revocable) is allowed for MCC 7322 loan EMI. (https://juspay.io/upi-merchant-stack/docs/mandates/register-mandate-intent, https://juspay.io/upi-consumer-stack/docs/customer-mandates/create-mandate). App-specific variants are `tez://upi/mandate`, `phonepe://mandate` and `paytmmp://mandate` (https://docs.solidgate.com/payments/alternative-payments/apms-overview/upi/upi-intent/).
- **Signed UPI QR**: a `sign=<signature>` parameter is appended to `upi://pay?...&orgId=..&sign=..`. See section C.

### A.2 Payload → risk → warning table

| Payload | Format (example) | Risk (evidence) | What QR Shield should warn (plain language) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WIFI (open / `nopass`)** | `WIFI:S:Railway_Free_WiFi;T:nopass;;` | Evil-twin APs that look like legitimate public Wi-Fi can capture traffic and serve phishing captive portals (https://securew2.com/blog/what-is-an-evil-twin-attack-in-wi-fi-and-how-can-i-protect-against-it, https://www.phishingbox.com/glossary/evil-twin-phishing). On iOS the camera recognises `WIFI:` and offers a one-tap join, and on Android a "Connect to network" chip appears (https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/97060, https://scanapp.org/blog/2026/06/01/how-to-connect-to-wifi-by-scanning-a-qr-code.html). CERT-In advises against banking or shopping on public Wi-Fi (https://www.deccanherald.com/india/avoid-public-wi-fi-cert-2029786, https://morungexpress.com/govt-cautions-citizens-against-using-public-wi-fi-for-transactions). There was a real evil-twin airport prosecution in Australia (https://www.cxodigitalpulse.com/?p=18561). | "This joins an OPEN Wi-Fi network named X. Anyone nearby can see your traffic. Don't do UPI or banking on it. If it asks for your phone number, OTP or card on a login page, stop." |
| **WIFI (`WEP`)** | `WIFI:T:WEP;S:..;P:..;;` | WEP is obsolete, insecure encryption (general knowledge; **UNVERIFIED** from a cited source in this doc). | "This network uses WEP, an old encryption that is easily broken. Treat it like open Wi-Fi." |
| **WIFI (lookalike public SSID)** | `WIFI:S:RailWire_Free;T:nopass;;` | Indian Railways' genuine station Wi-Fi SSID is **RailWire** (RailTel), and it logs you in with an OTP on a captive portal (https://www.faceofit.com/login-to-indian-railways-wifi/amp, https://tele.net.in/?p=6228). A QR code is not part of the normal flow (inference). I found no confirmed India railway or airport evil-twin incident, so **UNVERIFIED** that it happens in India. | "Real railway Wi-Fi is called exactly 'RailWire' and you pick it from Wi-Fi settings. You don't need a QR code. This name is similar but not identical." Keep a lookalike list (RailWire, airport names, 'Free', 'Airport_Free_WiFi', and so on). |
| **WIFI (`H:true` hidden)** | `WIFI:S:x;T:WPA;P:y;H:true;;` | A hidden SSID means the user can't see the network in the list to confirm it (inference). | "This adds a hidden network. You won't be able to see it to check it is real." |
| **WIFI (password exposed)** | any `P:` | The QR holds the password in plain text (https://scanapp.org/blog/2026/06/01/how-to-connect-to-wifi-by-scanning-a-qr-code.html). | Informational for shopkeepers: "Anyone who photographs this sticker gets your Wi-Fi password." |
| **tel:** | `tel:+919xxxxxxxxx` | (1) USSD codes inside `tel:` were historically auto-executed. A QR could trigger a factory reset on 2012-era Samsung dialers (https://www.infosecinstitute.com/resources/hacking/qr-code-ussd-attack, https://www.hkcert.org/blog/dialer-vulnerability-exists-in-some-android-phones-could-lead-to-data-damage). (2) India's **call-forwarding scam**: victims are told to dial `*401*<number>` or a code starting `21`, which diverts their calls, including bank OTP calls (PIB/DoT advisory 11 Jan 2024 via https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/malicious-calls-centre-warns-users-against-dialling-401-followed-by-unknown-mobile-number-580387). DoT ordered USSD call forwarding discontinued from 15 Apr 2024 (https://dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/28032024%20ussd.pdf), and the current status is **UNVERIFIED**. (3) Wangiri / premium-rate callbacks at Rs 50–200/min, often from +92 and other foreign prefixes (https://www.quickheal.com/blogs/attackers-trick-victims-into-calling-them-back-on-premium-rate-numbers/). | **Block, don't dial** if the number contains `*`, `#`, `%23`, or starts `*401`, `**21`, `*21`, `**61`/`62`/`67`: "This code changes your phone settings, for example call forwarding. Telecom companies never ask you to dial *401#." For a non-+91 country code: "International number (country X). Calls may cost a lot." For a mobile number labelled "bank" or "customer care": "Banks now *call* from 1600-series numbers. Get the helpline from the official app or website, not a sticker." |
| **sms: / SMSTO:** | `SMSTO:+91..:START 0` | Can pre-fill a message to a premium or short code, or send a "subscribe" or "verify" text from your SIM (inference). Indian DND registration uses SMS to 1909 (https://www.fast2sms.com/help/?p=876). | Show the recipient and full body. Flag 4–6 digit short codes and foreign numbers: "Sending this SMS may subscribe you to a paid service or share info from your SIM." |
| **mailto: / MATMSG** | `mailto:x@y?subject=..&body=..` | Pre-filled phishing or lure email. Low direct risk (inference). | Show the recipient domain. Flag mismatches, for example `sbi-help@gmail.com`: "This is not an official bank address." |
| **geo:** | `geo:19.07,72.87` | Mostly benign. Could lure the user to a meeting spot (inference). | Show the place. "Opens a map location only." |
| **vCard / MECARD** | `BEGIN:VCARD … TEL:… END:VCARD` | Contact poisoning, for example saving "SBI Customer Care" with a scammer's number (inference, no cited incident; **UNVERIFIED** as a documented India trend). Kaspersky blocks saving contacts that contain dangerous links (https://support.kaspersky.com/kaspersky-for-android/11.110/237265). | "This saves a contact named 'X Bank Support' with a personal mobile number. Banks don't share helplines this way." |
| **VEVENT (calendar)** | `BEGIN:VEVENT SUMMARY:.. DTSTART:..` | Calendar spam carrying a phishing link in the description or URL (inference). | Show the title, time and any URLs inside, and analyse those URLs. |
| **market:// / Play / App Store links** | `market://details?id=com.x` | Leads to app install. Remote-access apps such as AnyDesk and TeamViewer are used in UPI fraud, and GPay warns about them (https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/9851787?hl=en-GB). Scanner apps themselves have turned malicious, for example Barcode Scanner (10M+ installs) in 2021 (https://www.malwarebytes.com/?p=60171). | "This opens an app install page for `com.anydesk...`, a remote-control app. Scammers use it to watch your UPI PIN." Keep a list of remote-access and APK-sideload apps. |
| **intent://** | `intent://scan/#Intent;scheme=zxing;package=...;end` | Launches activities in installed Android apps. Intent-scheme attacks on browsers (cookie theft, UXSS) are documented (https://www.mbsd.jp/Whitepaper/IntentScheme.pdf, https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/intents). A QR-specific attack is **UNVERIFIED** and plausible. | "This opens app `<package>` directly. Only continue if you expected that app." Show the package, and flag `S.browser_fallback_url` that points to an unknown domain. |
| **App deep links (whatsapp://, wa.me, t.me)** | `https://wa.me/91xxxxxxxxxx?text=..` | Moves the conversation to chat, which helps social engineering such as task or job scams (inference). Messaging device-linking QR codes have been abused to hijack accounts: Russia-aligned actors used Signal "linked devices" QR codes in 2025 (https://thehackernews.com/2025/02/hackers-exploit-signals-linked-devices.html). | "Opens a WhatsApp chat with +91… and pre-types: '…'. Be careful if someone asks for money, OTP or a 'task fee'." |
| **QR login (WhatsApp Web style) / QRLJacking** | site shows QR → victim scans with app | The victim scans an attacker-relayed login QR, and the attacker gets the session (OWASP QRLJacking https://github.com/owasp/qrljacking; https://thehackernews.com/2016/07/qrljacking-hacking-qr-code.html). These sources date from 2016–2018, so current WhatsApp exposure is **UNVERIFIED**. | QR Shield can't see these, because they are scanned inside the messenger. Education card: "Only scan a 'Link device' QR that *you* opened on your own computer." |
| **otpauth://** | `otpauth://totp/Bank:me?secret=..` | Adds an authenticator entry. A risk mainly if someone tricks a user into adding attacker-controlled 2FA, or reveals that a secret was shared (inference, low). | "This adds a 2FA code for 'Issuer'. The secret is visible. Don't share screenshots of this QR." |
| **FIDO:/ (passkey cross-device)** | `FIDO:/1234567…` | Attempted phishing relay ("PoisonSeed", 2025). It was later retracted as a successful bypass because the proximity (BLE) check stopped it (https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/threat-actors-try-to-downgrade-fido2-mfa-auth-in-poisonseed-phishing-attack/, https://darkreading.com/remote-workforce/poisonseed-attacker-fido-keys). | "This is a sign-in request for a computer. Only approve it if you are signing in on a device in front of you right now." |
| **bitcoin: / ethereum:** | `bitcoin:bc1q…?amount=..` | Fake QR generators always output the scammer's wallet, with $45k+ lost (https://blog.knowbe4.com/novel-but-retrospectively-obvious-a-qr-code-generator-scam). The FTC's Dec 2023 QR warning was general, not crypto-specific (https://www.cnbc.com/2023/12/07/qr-codes-may-be-a-gateway-to-identity-theft-ftc-warns.html). | "Crypto payment to address bc1q…9f. Crypto transfers can't be reversed. Check the first and last 6 characters with the receiver over another channel." |
| **EMVCo / Bharat QR (`000201…`)** | `000201010211…26..0010A000000524 01..vpa@bank…5802IN…6304XXXX` | The same risks as `upi://pay`. Parsers may mis-read it. A CRC mismatch means the payload is corrupt or tampered (https://docs.docbits.com/administration-and-setup/settings/document-processing/module/bar-code-qr-code-extraction/emvco-mpm). | Parse the TLV, validate the CRC, and pull out the VPA (sub-tag 01), name (59), city (60) and amount (54). "CRC invalid: this QR may be edited." Then run the UPI checks. |
| **upi://mandate (AutoPay)** | `upi://mandate?...&recur=MONTHLY&am=..&rev=N` | Sets up a *recurring* debit. `rev=N` means it can't be cancelled (Juspay docs above). | "This sets up AUTOPAY: up to ₹X every month until DATE. It is not a one-time payment." Flag `rev=N` loudly. |

### A.3 Indian number patterns for tel:/sms: heuristics

| Pattern | Meaning | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `1600xxxxxx` / `1601ABCXXX` (10 digits) | Designated series for **service/transactional calls** by RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA-regulated entities and government. `1601` = financial and `1600` = government. Digits 5–6 = circle, digit 7 = operator. These are caller IDs for outgoing calls, **not a customer-care helpline**. | https://exotel.com/blog/1600-phone-number/, https://www.money9.com/news/exclusive/watch-out-for-calls-from-160-series-banks-or-govt-bodies-could-be-calling-you-138050.html |
| TRAI deadlines for 1600 | Direction of 19 Nov 2025. Banks by 1 Jan 2026, MFs/AMCs by 15 Feb 2026, brokers by 15 Mar 2026. 1600 calls cannot be tagged or blocked under TCCCPR (July 2026 clarification). | https://ianslive.in/trai-orders-mandatory-adoption-of-1600-series-numbers-by-banks-financial-services-insurance-firms--20251119152705, https://www.scconline.com/blog/?p=390749, https://telanganatoday.com/trai-clarifies-rules-for-1600-and-140-number-series-to-prevent-misinformation |
| `140xxx` | **Promotional/telemarketing** calls, any sector, blockable via DND. RBI notification of 17 Jan 2025 asks banks to use 1600xx for transactional calls and 140xx for promotional calls. | https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/telecom-regulatory-authority-of-india-clarifies-framework-for-1600-and-140-number-series/, https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/rbi-asks-banks-to-use-only-1600xx-series-phone-numbers-to-call-customers-for-transactional-purposes |
| `1909` | DND/NCPR registration and complaints (SMS `START 0`). | https://www.fast2sms.com/help/?p=876, https://plivo.com/blog/how-to-file-a-dnd-complaint-in-india |
| `1930` | National Cyber Crime Helpline (I4C), for reporting financial fraud. Report online at cybercrime.gov.in. | https://ps.keralapolice.gov.in/ernakulamruralcyber-ps/notifications/National-Cyber-Crime-Reporting-Helpline-No-1930-3338 |
| `*401*…`, `*21*…`, `**21*…` | Unconditional call-forwarding codes used in fraud. | https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/malicious-calls-centre-warns-users-against-dialling-401-followed-by-unknown-mobile-number-580387, https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/cybercrime-unit-warns-of-rising-ussd-call-forwarding-scam-targeting-bank-accounts-86755.htm |
| Foreign prefixes (+92, +234, +257, +265, +7 …) | Wangiri/premium callback sources reported. | https://www.quickheal.com/blogs/attackers-trick-victims-into-calling-them-back-on-premium-rate-numbers/, https://nairobinews.nation.africa/?p=164062 |
| Indian domestic premium-rate (IPR) numbers | **UNVERIFIED.** I could not find an authoritative current list of Indian premium-rate number ranges. Don't hardcode one; flag foreign codes and short codes instead. | — |

**Suggested scanner rule set (inference):**
1. `tel:` containing `*` or `#`: BLOCK.
2. Non-+91 country code: WARN (cost).
3. A "bank" label next to a 10-digit mobile number (6–9 prefix): WARN.
4. `1930`: INFO ("official cyber helpline").
5. `1600/1601`: INFO ("this is an outbound bank caller ID, not a helpline").
6. `sms:` to a 3–6 digit short code or foreign number: WARN, and show the body.

### B.3 What nobody (found) does: the QR Shield gap list

1. **UPI payload semantics before the payment app opens.** Telling the user "you are PAYING ₹X to `name`/`vpa`", "this is AUTOPAY (recurring, non-revocable)", or "a QR never *receives* money for you". No scanner in the table documents this. The UPI apps only act inside their own flow.
2. **Plain-language, multilingual reasons for each verdict.** Kaspersky, Trend and Norton give red/green or a reputation verdict. Atomic Asher claims "multilingual" but publishes no details.
3. **Non-URL payload risk rules.** For example `tel:` USSD or call-forwarding codes, open or lookalike Wi-Fi such as RailWire, `upi://mandate`, `intent://`, remote-access app installs, and FIDO or device-link QR codes. Competitors either show raw text or check URLs only.
4. **Shopkeeper verification mode.** No consumer tool lets a merchant scan their own standee and confirm the VPA still matches. The soundbox only reveals a swap after money is lost.
5. **Community reports** of tampered stickers or VPAs at a location. FRI and the Suspect Registry are bank-side and not open to the public.
6. **Offline heuristic fallback.** Cloud-reputation scanners need connectivity. ScamRakshak is offline, but it is for messages.

## C. Merchant QR tampering mitigations already in market

| Mitigation | What exists (cited) | Limits / how QR Shield complements |
|---|---|---|
| **Soundbox audio and visual confirmation** | Paytm Soundbox and Soundbox 2.0 (with screen), Google Pay SoundPod (https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/22/google-pay-takes-its-qr-sound-box-to-small-merchants-in-india-after-trial-run), Mswipe and others. In the Khajuraho case the merchant spotted the problem because credits were missing, then checked CCTV (https://thelogicalindian.com/watch-qr-code-scam-hits-12-madhya-pradesh-shops-fraudsters-divert-payments-overnight-to-their-accounts/). QR Shield's merchant mode can catch a swap **before** customers pay. |
| **Signed intent / signed QR (`sign=` parameter)** | NPCI UPI 2.0 (Aug 2018) introduced "Signed intent & QR". It tells the user whether the merchant is a verified UPI merchant and "negates the chances of QR tampering" (as quoted in secondary coverage of the press release, https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/press-releases/2018/NPCI%20launches%20Unified%20Payments%20Interface%202%200.pdf; the PDF text could not be extracted, so the quote is **UNVERIFIED** directly). QR Shield can still (a) note when `sign` is present or absent, and (b) never claim "verified" on its own. |
| **Dynamic QR** | Recommended for merchants by PayU: a per-transaction QR with an amount reduces the value of swapping static stickers (https://payu.in/blog/upi-fraud-prevention-ways-to-protect-your-business-from-scams/). | Most kiranas use static QR codes. |
